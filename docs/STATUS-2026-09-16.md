## 2. Rules you changed

Each one is a dated row in section 09 of `docs/protocol.html` and a commit
labelled `PRE-REGISTRATION CHANGE`. No trades had been collected, so nothing was
thrown away.

1. **Warm-up trades are kept**, flagged, and shown separately — not excluded.
   The first pass/fail check now arrives 30 trades sooner.
2. **Gap-open days are kept**, flagged, and reported on their own line.
3. **News sit-outs cover more releases**: PPI and retail sales join FOMC, CPI
   and the jobs report — about 53 mornings a year instead of 32.
4. **An FOMC day** is the announcement day of a scheduled meeting. Emergency
   actions don't count, because nobody could have planned around them.
5. **S1's retest is judged on the bar's close**, not its wick.
6. **The failed breakout (IB_FAIL) has rules**: it triggers when a break closes
   more than 2 points back inside, enters on a stop order 1 tick beyond that
   bar, and stops 1 point beyond the false break.
7. **S3 pulls back to the value-area edge** as well as to VWAP, and its stop
   sits beyond the first thin price below the pullback.
8. **A pullback must be a real pullback**: price has to come back to a level it
   had left, and one pullback gives one trade.
9. **One trade per IB edge per session, and never two positions in one setup.**
10. **SPY runs scale** every point threshold by the prior day's SPY vs S&P 500
    ratio.
11. **IB_FAIL is exploratory**: reported, never given a verdict, left out of the
    totals, and outside the significance correction.
12. **August 2026 is validation-only** and can never join a sample.

## 3. What the tooling gained

- **News calendar** built from FRED (CPI, jobs, PPI, retail sales) and the
  Fed's own FOMC calendar. It states the dates it covers; a session outside
  that range counts as *unknown*, not quiet.
- **Trade log** now records both the exit you took and the fixed 1:2 bracket
  result, plus the contract, the gap size and which checks failed. Statistics
  run on the bracket; your own exits are reported beside them.
- **Databento loader** saves each download and re-reads it instead of buying
  again, and confirms the delta sign from the quotes before anything runs.
- **Optimus Flow comparison** (`scripts/compare_optimus_export.py`) reads a
  History Exporter CSV and checks it against our bars.
- **Evaluator** reports exploratory setups without verdicts, drops
  validation-only periods, and shows gap days and exclusion reasons.

## 4. The August run

Bought 8.29M ES trades for $17.30 and ran the whole pipeline.

**It worked.** 22 sessions built. The delta sign checked out on 100% of
8,294,972 trades at the bid or ask. No ambiguous bars. The news sit-outs held
on the CPI and PPI days.

**It also caught a real bug.** Price wobbling across an IB edge counted as a
new break each time: one edge was sold six times in an hour, and 11 of 29
trades overlapped another open trade in the same setup. Your two rules fixed
it — the same month now produces **16 trades and no overlaps**.

**Independently cross-checked.** Your Optimus Flow export (a different data
feed) matched our bars on volume for **1,380 of 1,381 minutes**, with every
close identical. The one mismatch is the export's last bar, which absorbs the
rest of the requested window.

Because the rules were written after reading those trades, the month is
validation data. The evaluator refuses to count it.

## 5. What the numbers say about testing the setups

From those 22 sessions: **10 S1, 5 IB_FAIL, 1 S3** — about 0.45 S1 trades per
session.

- **S1 needs roughly 130 sessions** (about six months of data) to reach its
  first pass/fail checkpoint at 60 trades.
- **Data costs**: about **$1.00 per session** for the full trades-plus-quotes
  data, or about **$0.60 per session** for trades only. The quotes matter only
  for the delta-sign check, which can be re-confirmed with a single such day
  per contract. At $0.60, roughly 130 sessions costs about $78 — inside the
  remaining budget. At $1.00 it is not.
- **S3 will not get there.** At about one trade per 22 sessions, even 150
  sessions would give ~7 trades. The bar-level screen cannot reach a verdict on
  S3 at any realistic cost, so S3 stays a manual-replay setup. Loosening its
  conditions to raise the count would be fitting the rules to the data, not
  evidence.

   `python scripts/fetch_news_calendar.py` and
   `python scripts/fetch_reference_closes.py`.
3. **Write down which sessions the study will use, and commit that list**
   before buying them — so the pool is chosen ahead of the results, not after.

**Then, to collect a sample:**

4. **Decide the data schema.** Trades-only roughly doubles the sessions per
   dollar and is what makes S1's first checkpoint affordable. It needs a small
   loader change (the loader currently always asks for quotes).
5. **Buy and run.** Every purchase gets a price quote first and your go-ahead;
   downloads are saved and never re-bought.

**Open, not blocking:**

- **The delta sign versus Optimus Flow.** Your export had no delta column, so
  this is still unconfirmed. Either export a chart with a delta indicator, or
  read the cumulative delta at one minute off the screen and compare. It
  matters because S2 and S4 are hand-logged in that platform, and both series
  must mean the same thing by "delta confirmed".
- **Rule variants.** If you want to test more than one version of a setup, the
  safe shape is: specify every variant in advance, blind to results; commit
  them; run them all on sessions nobody has looked at; and report all of them
  rather than the best. Choosing a winner from data already seen would produce
  one whichever variants were tried — with ten trades, the best of eight
  worthless variants still averages about +1R, against a +0.15R bar for
  "interesting".

## 7. Things that would quietly ruin the study

- Tuning any threshold against data already looked at.
- Pooling trades from before and after a rule change.
- Counting the August month in a sample.
- Hand-replaying a session that was already read in a generated run.
- Treating IB_FAIL's result as a verdict.
