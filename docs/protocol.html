MES Replay Protocol

Five order-flow setups written as mechanical rules, with the logging schema, futility gates and sample-size math needed to tell a real edge from a run of luck. Pre-register before you replay; the rules are what's being tested, not your judgment on the day.

01The number that governs everything

Read this before designing anything else, because it determines how many setups you can honestly evaluate.

Design constraint

A 1:2 R system with a 40% win rate has an R-multiple standard deviation near 1.5. At that dispersion, confirming a genuinely good edge of +0.25R takes roughly 360 trades at 80% power with the multiple-comparison correction applied. A marginal +0.15R edge takes over a thousand. You cannot confirm five setups. You can screen five and confirm one.

So the protocol is asymmetric by design. Rejection is cheap and fast; confirmation is expensive and reserved for the one or two survivors. That is not a compromise — it is the only structure the variance permits.

Read ES, trade MES. The micro book is thinner and its footprint clusters are correspondingly noisier. Every order-flow condition below is evaluated on the ES chart and ES DOM; MES is the execution vehicle only.

03Replay discipline

The rules that keep Market Replay from quietly becoming hindsight confirmation.

  1. Draw dates from a pre-committed pool.

    Randomly sample sessions across at least three different volatility regimes. Never replay a date you traded live or already reviewed — you will remember the outcome and you will not notice that you remember it.

  2. Start the tape at 09:00 ET, not at the setup.

    Load the session cold. No scrolling ahead, no glancing at the daily bar, no checking where the close landed. If the chart shows one bar past the current one, the session is contaminated — discard it.

  3. Replay at 1× to 2×, and place real orders through the DOM.

    Speed suppresses the hesitation that costs you fills live. Use bracket orders with the same defaults you would use with money on.

  4. One decision per bar, no re-runs.

    If you pause to think, the bar is over. Rewinding to re-take a trade invalidates the session for the entire study, not just that trade.

  5. Log a mechanical exit alongside your managed exit.

    Record the fixed 1:2 outcome for every trade regardless of how you actually managed it. The mechanical series is what the statistics run on; the managed series measures your discretion, and mixing them makes both unreadable.

  6. Mark the first 30 trades of each setup as burn-in.

    Your reading skill is non-stationary across a six-month study — the early trades and late trades are not draws from the same distribution. Burn-in trades stay in the primary analysis, flagged, and the result is also reported without them so the drift is visible (amended — see §09).

05The five setups

Every condition is binary and checkable on the tape. If a condition requires a judgment call you can't write down, it isn't a condition — it's a hope, and it belongs in notes.

S1

Initial balance break and retest

≈1.2 / session

Hypothesis: the 09:30–10:00 ET range establishes early accepted value. A break that survives a retest indicates inventory being repriced rather than a liquidity probe — and the stops resting beyond the IB are the fuel.

Trigger
  • IB range between 0.25% and 0.75% of index level — excludes both dead and blown-out openings
  • Break occurs between 10:00 and 11:30 ET
  • ES cumulative delta makes a new session extreme in the break direction within 3 bars of the break
  • Retest holds the IB edge for one full volume bar without closing back inside by more than 2 points
Entry
Limit at the IB edge ± 1 tick, on the retest only. No chasing the initial break.
Stop
1 point beyond the retest swing. Floor 4 pts, cap 8 pts — if structure demands more than 8, stand down.
Target
Mechanical series: 2R. Managed: half at 1R, runner to IB edge + 1.0 × IB range or a session VWAP break.
Stand down
FOMC / CPI / NFP days before 10:30 ET — FOMC meaning the statement day of a scheduled meeting, releases meaning the day they actually came out (clarified — see §09). Gap opens beyond 1% of prior close are a different regime — traded and flagged, and reported separately (amended — see §09).
Known failure modeThe false break that reverses back through the entire IB. Log those as IB_FAIL with the same schema — the mirror trade may carry more edge than the setup itself, and you'll only find out if you record it.
S2

Value-area edge to VPOC

≈0.7 / session

Hypothesis: on rotational days, responsive participants defend the prior session's value-area edges and price mean-reverts toward the point of control.

Trigger
  • Open inside the prior day's value area — this is the day-type filter, and it is mandatory
  • Price tags prior VAH or VAL after 10:00 ET
  • Footprint absorption: ≥2 consecutive clusters with strong one-way aggression and no new extreme
  • ES DOM shows resting size ≥3× the mean of the surrounding 10 levels, and it does not pull as price arrives
Entry
Limit 1–2 ticks inside the level, after the second failed push. Never the first.
Stop
1.5 points beyond the absorption zone extreme, cap 6 pts.
Target
Prior day's VPOC. Time stop: scratch at breakeven if price hasn't traveled 1R within 20 minutes.
Stand down
Open outside value. One-timeframing on 30-min bars. Delta trending with price.
Known failure modeThis bleeds badly on the ~30% of sessions that trend, and the losses cluster — you will take three of them on the same day. The entire question is whether balance-day winners pay for trend-day losses, so every result for S2 must be reported conditional on day type. An unconditional mean here is meaningless.
S3

VWAP pullback continuation

≈0.6 / session

Hypothesis: on trend days, execution algorithms work large orders around VWAP; pullbacks into it get filled rather than reversed. This is where the large R multiples live.

Day gate
All three, confirmed before any entry: open outside prior value; one-timeframing on 30-min bars for 2 consecutive bars; session cumulative delta at a session extreme with price.
Trigger
  • Pullback to session VWAP ± 2 points, or to the developing value-area edge
  • Declining volume on the pullback versus the impulse leg
  • Counter-delta on the pullback under 40% of the impulse delta — fading, not reversing
Entry
Choose one and pre-register it: limit at VWAP, or stop-entry 1 tick beyond the pullback bar's extreme. Mixing them mid-study destroys the sample.
Stop
Beyond the low-volume node under the pullback. Floor 5 pts, cap 10 pts.
Target
Mechanical: 2R. Managed: trail on 30-min structure. A fixed 1R target here throws away the setup's entire reason for existing.
Stand down
After 15:00 ET. Any day where VWAP has been crossed more than 3 times — that's a balance day in a trend costume.
Known failure modeMisclassifying a balance day as a trend day in the first hour. The 30-min confirmation is slow on purpose. Anticipating it converts this setup into S2 executed backwards, which is the worst trade on this page.
S4

HVN absorption reversal

≤1 / session

Hypothesis: a passive participant with real size defends a price level, and aggressive flow exhausts against it. The purest order-flow trade here and the least likely to be real.

Trigger
  • Price at a high-volume node from the prior 5 sessions' composite profile
  • ≥3 clusters where one-side aggression exceeds 65% of cluster volume with price advancing under 2 ticks
  • DOM Surface shows the resting size persisting through at least two full refreshes — not a flash
  • Cumulative delta makes a new extreme while price does not
Entry
Stop-entry 1 tick beyond the first cluster that reverses the imbalance. Never a limit — this setup requires proof, not anticipation.
Stop
1 point beyond the absorption extreme, cap 5 pts. If it needs more than 5, the read was wrong.
Target
2R, or the nearest opposing HVN, whichever is closer.
Stand down
First 5 and last 15 minutes of RTH. Any scheduled release minute. Thin tape manufactures false absorption signatures.
Known failure modeSpoofing — displayed size that never trades. The persistence requirement is your only defense and it is an imperfect one. Note also that replay reconstructs the book imperfectly, so S4 is the setup where replay results are least transferable to live. Test it last, and treat a positive result with more suspicion than a negative one.
S5

Overnight inventory correction

≈0.4 / session

Hypothesis: when the overnight session leaves positioning skewed to one extreme, the RTH open corrects the imbalance before establishing a direction.

Trigger
  • At 09:30, price sits in the top or bottom 15% of the overnight (18:00–09:30 ET) range
  • Overnight volume above the 20-day median — a thin overnight drift is noise, not inventory
Entry
First 5-minute bar that closes against the extreme, 09:30–10:00 ET only.
Stop
Beyond the overnight extreme, cap 8 pts.
Target
Overnight midpoint. Hard time stop at 10:30 ET.
Stand down
Gap opens entirely beyond the overnight range — that is a different event. 08:30 ET data days.
Known failure modeOn genuine trend days the "extreme" is the beginning of the move, not a correction. Expect a weak standalone expectancy. Test S5 as a covariate rather than a strategy: log on_range_pos on every S1 and S3 trade and check whether overnight positioning predicts their outcomes. A feature that improves two setups is worth more than a marginal sixth strategy.

06Gates and stopping rules

Pre-registered, checked at fixed sample sizes, applied without appeal.

The gate is a one-sided futility bound: kill the setup when its upper 80% confidence bound on mean R falls below the minimum expectancy worth trading (+0.15R). This rejects dead setups quickly while rarely discarding a live one.

CheckpointKill if mean R below Kills a true +0.25R setupKills a true +0.40R setupKills a true 0R setup
n = 60−0.0138.7%1.7%47%
n = 150+0.0474.9%0.2%65%

Assumes σ = 1.5R. Roughly one in eight genuinely good setups gets killed across both gates — the price of not spending two years on a mirage.

Promotion

A setup passes both gates and reaches the confirmation sample (see §01) before it earns live capital, and then at one contract for 40 sessions regardless of how good the replay numbers look. Replay omits slippage, hesitation, and the specific feeling of watching real money move — none of which are in the sample you just collected.

Reporting instrument

R-multiples are right-skewed and clustered within sessions, so normal-theory intervals are wrong twice over. Report the session-block bootstrap: resample whole sessions with replacement, recompute the mean, take the 2.5/97.5 percentiles. Trades within one session share a regime and share your state of mind that morning; treating them as independent will make every interval look about 30% tighter than it is.

07Sequencing

Test order is chosen by expected effect size, not by which setup is most interesting.

OrderSetupWhy here
1S3 VWAP_CONTLargest plausible effect and best R structure, so it needs the fewest trades to resolve. Also the easiest day gate to check mechanically.
2S1 IB_BREAKHighest frequency, so the sample accumulates fastest. Collect the IB_FAIL mirror at the same time for free.
3S2 LEVEL2LEVELNeeds day-type conditioning, which needs volume — run it after you've built the classification habit on S3.
4S5 ON_INVENTORYFree: the covariate is already logged on every S1 and S3 trade. Evaluate as a feature first.
5S4 ABSORPTIONLast. Highest skill requirement, worst replay fidelity, most likely to be a mirage. Your reading also improves most while you study it, which confounds the sample.

Run one setup at a time. Running all five concurrently multiplies the correction, dilutes the per-setup sample, and — the real cost — makes it impossible to tell whether a bad stretch was the setup or your attention split five ways.

08Analysis

The evaluation script implements the cost model, the block bootstrap, and the gates above.

# after each block of replay sessions
python mes_replay_eval.py trades.csv --min-expectancy 0.15 --alpha 0.01

# reports per setup:
#   n, win rate, mean R, sd, net P&L, avg risk in dollars
#   session-block bootstrap 95% CI on mean R, and P(mean <= 0)
#   gate verdict: KILL / CONTINUE, and trades still needed to confirm
#   breakdown by day type and by exit reason
#   portfolio view: daily P&L distribution and max drawdown

Run it on a fixed cadence — every 20 sessions, say — not whenever a stretch feels good or bad. Checking the numbers after a losing week and again after a winning one is an informal sequential test with no correction, and it will find whatever you are hoping for.

This is a testing protocol, not trading advice. MES is leveraged; losses can exceed the intended risk on gaps, and the published research on retail day trading finds a low single-digit percentage of participants profitable net of costs over multi-year windows. The purpose of running this protocol is as much to find out whether any of these setups clear that bar as it is to trade them.
